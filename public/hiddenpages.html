Getting started

Unknown Island is a shared island for you and your friends. Everyone plays on the same island, and it keeps going when nobody is online: days pass, fires burn out, things regrow, the tide comes in and the fog creeps back.

  • The first time, choose I have an invite, pick a name and password, and type the invite code (ask Thaqif). After that just log in.
  • New arrivals see a short intro. You can watch it again from Settings.
  • When you log off, your frog leaves the island and your stats freeze until you come back. You reappear where you left.
  • If nobody has played for a while the server is asleep; the first visit can take up to a minute to wake it.
First night: find fresh water inland (the spring), gather wood and stone, and build a campfire (F) before dark.

The screen

  • Top left: the day number and time of day, a countdown (Night in 3:12 / Dawn in 1:05), the moon phase and any weather, then your bars: Health, Food, Water, Energy and Dread. The line underneath warns you when you're cold, warm by a fire, exhausted, knocked down or in thick fog.
  • Top right: who's on the island (in their map colours) and the minimap.
  • Bottom left: your eight item slots and your tools.
  • Bottom middle: what E will do right now.
  • No crosshair (with a mouse): it stays out of sight, since whatever E would use already has its label. It will only show when there’s something to hit.
  • Red glow at the screen edges: your health is below half (stronger as it drops, pulsing below a quarter), or you just got hurt.
  • Dropped sacks glow white on the ground so they're easy to spot.

Food, water and health

  • Hunger and thirst only go down while you move ( food and water per second). Standing still costs nothing. Sprinting makes you hungrier.
  • At zero food or water you lose health a second (each).
  • The cold: at night, away from a fire or lit lantern, you lose health a second.
  • Healing: with food and water above and nothing hurting you, you heal a second.
  • You start with .

Water

  • Springs: fresh water, + a drink. The main spring is south of the big hill; there are others in hollows around the island.
  • The sea: drinking it makes you thirstier (). Boil it clean in a bucket instead (below).
  • Rain refills your water slowly while it lasts.

Clean water and buckets

Buckets turn seawater into drinking water, so you don't have to walk back to the spring.

  1. Hold the empty bucket (1–8), wade into the sea and press E to fill it.
  2. Walk to a lit fire and press E to set it on to boil. A countdown floats over the fire. It only boils while the fire burns (E at the fire adds wood), and it keeps boiling while nobody's online.
  3. When it says Clean water ready, press E at the fire to take it.
  4. Hold it in your hand and hold E to drink (a ring fills, like eating): drinks of + water.

Each boil wears the bucket by one (the green bar under its slot). A worn-out bucket breaks once it's emptied. You can carry up to , and you can drop a full bucket (G) for a friend.

Moving, jumping and sitting

  • Energy: sprinting uses energy a second; it comes back a second after a short rest. Run out and you're exhausted: slower, no sprinting or jumping, until you're back to .
  • Jumping: tap Space for a hop ( energy). Hold it to crouch and charge: a full charge (about half a second) leaps three times as high and carries you forward, for three times the energy. Friends see you crouch and leap.
  • Standing on things: land on a rock, ore rock, bush or a low tree top and you stand on it; walk off the edge to drop. Jump higher than something and you pass over it. Big trees are too tall, and their leaves are solid: jump up under them and you bump your head.
  • Bumping: other frogs are solid; you can't walk through each other.
  • Sitting (V): sit on the ground with your knees up. Moving or jumping gets you up. Sitting by a fire calms dread twice as fast.
  • Wading in the sea is slow, and you can't jump in water.
  • Climbing: walk into one of the tall palms, or into a cliff (ground too steep to walk up), and you grab on. W / S climb up and down, A / D go round the trunk or along the cliff, Space lets go. It costs energy a second while you climb ( just hanging on); run out and you slide back down. Keep climbing at the top of a palm to pull yourself up onto its leaves; at the top of a cliff you step out onto the ground above.
  • Gliding: falling from at least m up (off a palm, a cliff or a tree top), hold Space and your cloak catches the air: you sink slowly and drift forward, steering with the walking keys, for energy a second. Let go of Space to drop.

Day, night, the moon and weather

  • A full day is : of daylight (with short sunrise and sunset) and of night. The countdown in the top-left tells you how long until night falls or dawn comes.
  • Night is cold, the fog rolls in and the Stilled walk. Stay by a fire.
  • The day number goes up at sunrise. Coconuts and berries refill, the tide comes in, dug patches settle.

The moon

The moon turns every days. Day 1 was a full moon. The Drowning Moon (the dark one) brings the thickest fog of the cycle, more Stilled, and lanterns drink their oil × as fast; you'll get a warning at dusk. Full moons bring rare finds: moon moths, glowing mushrooms, lantern fish.

Tools wear out

A tool is something you carry, one to a slot. Hold it (1–8) to use it: chopping with the axe in your hand gives 2 wood, mining with a pickaxe in hand gives 2 stone (and ore needs one), and digging needs the shovel in hand. Just having one in your bag isn't enough.

Every use wears it down; the bar under its slot shows how much is left, and it warns you when it gets low. Worn out, it breaks, and you keep one of what it was made of. You can make another whenever you like.

Mending: hold a worn tool and press E at a lit clay hearth. It costs about half of what the tool took to make, and it's as good as new. Tools stay with you when you're knocked down or die; dropped in a sack, they keep their wear.

Sleeping by a hearth

Hold V beside a lit clay hearth, yours or a friend's (a tap only sits you down), and once the ring fills your frog curls up under a blanket the colour of its cloak, eyes shut, little z's drifting up. A few seconds later: "The fire will remember you." Sleeping refills your energy twice as fast.

  • From then on, when you're knocked down or you collapse, you wake beside that hearth instead of on the beach.
  • If it has gone out, you still wake there, but cold and uneasy. Keep it fed.
  • If it's gone altogether (the fog can take a fire), you're back on the beach where you first arrived.
  • Your hearth shows on the map as a flag in your colour; friends' flags are fainter. A small pennant flies on the hearth itself. Friends can all sleep at one hearth and make it a camp.
  • Without a hearth, being knocked down leaves you where you fell, as before.

Inventory, holding and giving

  • You have 8 slots, numbered 1–8. Each kind of thing keeps its slot; each bucket has its own.
  • You carry things in hotbar slots (1–8, along the bottom of the screen) and a bag of more (I). A slot holds up to of one thing ( of a food, and one bucket). New things fill the hotbar first, then the bag. Picking an empty hotbar slot (or stepping onto one with Q or the wheel) leaves your hands empty.
  • In the bag: drag a stack onto an empty slot to move it, onto the same thing to add to it, or onto something else to swap them. Drag with the right mouse button to take half. Shift+click sends a stack across, between the bag and the hotbar. Drag it off the page to drop it. On a phone, tap a stack and then tap where it goes; the buttons under the bag take half, send it across, eat it or drop it.
  • A full bag: whatever doesn't fit is left in a sack at your feet. Picking up a sack takes what fits and leaves the rest in it.
  • Press a number (or Q to cycle) to hold that item. Your frog carries it in its hand and friends can see it.
  • G drops one of the held item in a sack at your feet; Shift+G drops the whole stack. Drops next to a sack go into it.
  • Giving things to a friend: drop them, and your friend picks up the sack with E. Sacks glow and say what's inside, and show on the map.

The fog and dread

The fog is the island's enemy. By day it sits out over the sea; at dusk it rises and fills the low ground and valleys (the hills stay clear on a normal night). Light cuts clear circles out of it: fires and lit lanterns.

The Stilled

  • Pale, faceless figures that stand in the fog at night.
Spoiler How they behave (click to show)
  • They only move when nobody is looking at them. Keep your camera on them and they freeze.
  • They can't enter light or clear air: fires and lit lanterns make safe circles.
  • They're drawn to frogs who are alone or frightened.
  • If one reaches you, you're knocked down: − health, + dread, and half of what you carry spills into a sack on the ground where you fell. Go back for it… carefully.
  • They can be fought: it takes a few blows (blows in firelight, lantern light or by torchlight, and silver, hit twice as hard), and one that breaks scatters into fog. It forms again where it broke, later that night.

Fighting

  • Attack: with mouse-look, click; or press R; on a phone, the Attack button. You swing what's in your hand where you're aiming: a weapon, an axe or pickaxe, or your fists.
  • Heavy swing: hold the attack for a moment, then let go. It hits × as hard and costs energy.
  • Roll: double-tap Shift (on a phone, swipe the Attack button). For s nothing can touch you, for energy. Every creature's attack is marked on the ground before it lands: that's your moment.
  • Light is a weapon: holding a torch, or close to a lit fire or lantern, your blows carry light. The things of the fog don't like it.
  • Down, not dead: a blow that would kill you leaves you down instead. A friend can pick you up by holding E beside you within s. Otherwise you come to at your hearth (or on the beach), aching.

Torches

Make torches from wood and seeds (the recipe book has them). Hold one (keys 1–8) and it's lit, wherever you are:

  • It lights the ground round you (at night it's the brightest thing about), and friends see it too.
  • It pushes the fog back about steps round you, and keeps you warm at night (only close by: a campfire warms a wider circle).
  • It burns down while you hold it: about seconds, then the next one in your stack lights.
  • Plant it: with a torch in hand, G plants it upright in the ground in front of you. It lights, warms and holds back the fog there for anyone, for about seconds, then burns out. Not in water or underground. (Shift+G still drops the whole stack in a sack.)
  • Take it back: walk up to a planted torch and press E, with what's left of it.

Stone lanterns

Old stone lanterns stand all over the island. Light one and it pushes the fog back in a wide circle, warms you, and makes a safe camp.

  • They burn lamp oil (made from in the recipe book). One oil lasts about ; E with oil tops a lit lantern up.
  • Normal lanterns clear a circle steps wide; the four great lanterns on the hills clear , but need oil from different frogs to light.

The fog fights back

  • A lantern that runs dry goes cold and needs a fresh offering.
  • Once cold, the fog takes its clearing back bit by bit over days. Relight it in time and nothing is lost.
  • When the fog fully takes an area back, some trees, rocks and bushes there are swallowed (they regrow later), fires are put out and taken, and sacks are dragged deeper into the fog.
  • The longer an area has been held, the more oil its lantern burns (up to as much). The Drowning Moon drains all lanterns faster.
  • All of this carries on while nobody is playing. Your inventory and journal are never touched.

Travelling by lantern light

  • Stand in a lit lantern's light and you'll remember it: you can find your way back to that light.
  • At a lit lantern, E shows the other lit lanterns you remember, how far and which way, and what it costs: 1 lamp oil for every steps (at least ). Pick one and you're there. You can still add oil to the lantern from the same list.
  • Both lanterns have to be lit: a cold lantern can't be travelled to or from. Keep the ones you rely on fed.
  • Not from a cave, while knocked down, or dead.

The carving stones

Three old standing stones (by the beach camp, near the main spring, and on the highest ground) carry carvings that change by themselves. Read one with E.

Spoiler What the stones do (click to show)

Sometimes the island itself seems to be asking for something: an offering, lights in a certain place, frogs gathered at night…

  • Read a stone with E. If it wants something you carry, you can leave it at the stone's foot. Tally marks show how far along the group is.
  • Each request has a few days. Answer it and something good happens: the fog holds back for a night, something is left at the stone, a lantern lights itself, a note appears on the board…
  • Ignore it and the fog presses harder for a while, and lanterns burn faster.
  • A new carving appears at dawn. Put your ear to a stone at night and listen.

There are several different requests so far; more may be added.

Spoiler The stones tell a story (click to show)

The stones don't only ask at random. Each part of the island has its own run of requests, carved one after another, and together they tell a story.

  • When one is answered, the next in the run is carved at the following dawn.
  • Ignore one and the fog presses as usual, then the same request comes back at the next dawn. The story waits for you.
  • The Landing's run has five requests. After the last one, something comes up out of the sea. The stones will tell you when and where.
  • Between runs, the stones ask for other things, as before.

Tides

Every sunrise the sea takes back what it left and washes new things onto the beaches: driftwood, crates of seeds and oil, fish, shells and sea glass. Pick them up with E. After storms there's more.

Some things the tide brings shouldn't be there. At most one a day.

The driftwood board

By the first lantern near the beach. Read it and pin notes for everyone (one every 30 seconds). Now and then a note appears that nobody wrote.

Calling out

Lost your friends? Press C (or Call on a phone) to call out. Anyone within metres hears a frog's call coming from your direction (muffled if one of you is in a cave), and sees a bubble over your head. Everyone further away is told which way you are. For seconds a ring on everyone's map marks where you called from. You can call once every seconds.

What friends see

Your cloak colour, patches, hood up or down (T), what you're holding, sitting, crouching to jump and leaping, and speech bubbles.

The map

Press M or click the minimap. Land is coloured by type (beach, meadow, forest, hills, peak, spring pools, sea). Markers: a droplet for springs, a diamond for lanterns (bright when lit, bigger for great ones), a standing stone for carving stones, a square for the board, a flame for fires (grey when out), and a sack for dropped things. You're the arrow labelled You; friends are coloured dots with their names. The legend under the map lists everything.

Moving the map: drag it to look around, scroll (or pinch) to zoom, or use the buttons: + and − to zoom, Me to centre on yourself, All to see everything.

Charting: beyond the Landing the map starts blank. It fills in wherever anyone has walked, for everyone, and stays filled in. The minimap shows the land around you.

Caves

Some of the island is hollow. Walk into a cave's mouth and you're under the ground, no loading, and your friends see you go in. Inside there's no fog, no cold wind and none of the Stilled, but it gets dark quickly.

  • The Dark: deep in a cave with no light, your dread climbs, faster than a night out in the open. A light of your own (or a friend's close by) keeps it away.
  • Torches: made from wood and seeds (the recipe book has them). Hold one (keys 1 to 8) and it lights the way for you and everyone near you. A torch burns for about seconds in your hand underground(anywhere), then the next one lights.
  • You can't light a fire underground, and nothing up on the ground is in reach from down there.
  • If you leave the island while underground, you come back just outside the cave's mouth.
  • Anything you say in chat from underground reaches the people up above muffled, with words lost. Whoever is down there with you hears it clearly.
Spoiler The sea cave (click to show)

Under the low hill on the east shore of the Landing there's a sea cave. The way in dips below the sea, so it's under water most of the time: the tide goes out twice a day, and for a few minutes around each low tide you can wade through the dip into a wide chamber with a side passage. Watch the water. When the tide turns it fills the cave from the bottom up, and once it's deep enough where you stand the sea drags you out onto the sand.

Spoiler What lives in the sea cave (click to show)

The Crawler. Pale like the Stilled, but three times their size and all long arms and legs, it hangs from the roof deep in the cave and never leaves it. It can't see; it hunts by sound. Running, calling out and talking carry a long way down there, and anyone who walks right up to it is found anyway.

  • When it hears you it screams: your dread jumps, the ground shakes, and at least you know where it is.
  • Before it lunges, a strip is marked on the ground in front of it. Get out of the strip. Caught, you're knocked down and dragged deeper into the dark.
  • It hates light: come near it with a lit torch and it shrinks back and slinks away. It won't cross water, so when the tide comes in it clings to the roof and waits.
  • Being near it frightens you, even when it's quiet.

Beyond the Landing

The island you arrived on is only the Landing. A sandy neck on its north shore leads to much more land: about five kilometres of it, twenty minutes' walk end to end.

On a clear day you can see about two kilometres (one on a phone): from the Landing you can see the Stairs climbing away to the north. Land still behind the Veil looks pale and washed out from far away.

Most of it is behind the Veil, a wall of fog you can see banked up along the border. Walk into it and it turns you around (and it frightens you). On the map, land behind the Veil is fogged over and hatched.

Spoiler How the Veil lifts (click to show)

Each land opens when the one before it is finished: its run of carvings answered, and what comes after them dealt with. The Veil lifts at the next dawn, and stays lifted.

Spoiler What's out there (click to show)
  • The Stairs, straight north: grassy terraces climbing to about 220 m.
  • The Weeping Wood, to the north-east: thick forest with a wide river valley.
  • The Mire, to the north-west: flat, wet ground full of pools.
  • The Hollow, a crater further north, and the Teeth beyond it: mountains to about 600 m with snow on top.
  • The Ashen Shore, on the north-west coast, around an old volcano.

Each land is still bare and waiting for its own plants, creatures and story (the Stairs have theirs: below).

The Stairs

Grassy terraces climbing north from the Landing, windy and open, with pines on the high steps and the tumbledown walls of somebody's old village. Things you'll only find here:

  • Flint nodules in the turf: dark lumps with a chalky crust. Prise them out with E (a pickaxe gets two). Back in days.
  • Healing herbs: low rosettes with tiny white flowers. New leaves in day.
  • Flax: thin stems with sky-blue flowers, on the lower steps. Grows again in days.
  • Old walls of brick: work bricks loose with E (a pickaxe gets two). A wall gives three loads, then crumbles away, and the rubble turns up again in days.
  • Tin ore high on the steps: pale blue-grey flecks in the rock, mined like copper.
  • Standing stones, taller than three frogs, with worn marks near the top.
  • Their own bugs: the stone beetle, the terrace grasshopper and (rarely) something that only flies at night, high up.

What flint, herbs, flax and tin are for comes with tools that wear out and the bronze tier.

Spoiler The Leaning (click to show)

The Stairs have their own Stilled. The Leaning are tall, pale and thin, and they stand leaning back into the wind, quite still. They only move when it gusts: then the wind carries them, and they can steer a little towards you, but never against it. One that reaches you in a gust throws you down and shoves you downwind, down the steps.

  • They come out at night, upwind of whoever is on the Stairs (more of them for someone alone), and are gone by morning.
  • Being near one frightens you.
  • Shelter is the answer: with a standing stone or an old wall right beside you, on the side the wind comes from, they can't touch you, and go on past.
Spoiler The old mine (click to show)

Somebody dug into one of the terrace walls on the east side of the Stairs: a narrow shaft that slopes gently down into a wider chamber, with a side gallery. It's dark (bring a torch), and dry.

Spoiler The Stairs' carvings (click to show)

Once the Stairs are open, the carving stones ask for things from up there, in order: six flint, then eight bricks to mend the houses on the steps, then two of you down the old mine and out again, then six healing herbs, and last, catching the wind moth that flies high on the steps at night. What waits after that is still to come.

Getting hurt and dying

  • Hunger, thirst, the cold and the Stilled all hurt you. The screen edges glow red below half health.
  • A creature's blow never kills you outright: it leaves you down, for a friend to pick up (see Fighting).
  • If your health hits zero you collapse. You wake up back on the beach (or by the hearth you last slept at) with fresh stats: you keep your tools, but lose everything you were carrying.

Settings

Esc or the cog button: change any key, look sensitivity and invert, turn the creepy sounds on or off, graphics quality (Low for older phones), a how-to for chat and items, rewatch the intro, log out.

Changelog

Newest first.

  • Torches above ground: a torch in hand lights the way, pushes the fog back and keeps you warm, anywhere, and burns down. G plants it in the ground as a small light for everyone; E takes it back.
  • The sun and moon glow: a warm halo and slow-turning beams round the sun, a silvery halo round the moon (brighter the fuller it is). Both dim in rain and storms.
  • Fixed: walking off a cliff now falls instead of jumping you straight to the bottom, and the camera follows you down a long fall instead of showing the ground; the sleeping blanket's edge by your head falls onto the ground.
  • Fighting: swords, a spear, a club and a sling; heavy swings, rolling out of the way, and being picked up by a friend when you're down. The Stilled can be broken into fog, for a while.
  • Fixed: the sun and moon no longer rise up through faraway hills; and the sleeping blanket now covers your front up to the chin.
  • Search the Hidden Pages: type in the box above the contents (or press /) and only what mentions it stays, highlighted. Spoilers stay closed, with a tag when something inside matches. Esc clears it.
  • Fixed: Esc with a window open (the recipe book, the journal, the bag...) only closes it. With nothing open, Esc opens settings.
  • Sleeping is a hold: by a lit hearth, tap V to sit and hold it to sleep there. The blanket now tucks in under your chin.
  • A tidier start screen: the "What's new" box and the Hidden Pages link are gone from it. The Hidden Pages are still in Settings, and this changelog is where changes are listed.
  • Travel by lantern light: stand in a lit lantern's light to remember it; at a lit lantern, E takes you to another lit one you remember, for lamp oil by distance.
  • Tools wear out: tools are carried in your bag and used from your hand. They wear down with every use, break into one of their material, and can be mended at a lit hearth.
  • The Stairs come alive: flint, healing herbs, flax, old brick walls, standing stones and tin; three bugs of their own; something that comes out on windy nights; an old mine; and the Stairs' own run of carvings.
  • Sleeping looks like sleeping: you curl up by a hearth in half a second, under a thick blanket in your cloak's colour that drapes over you and spills onto the ground, eyes closed, with z's floating up out of your head.
  • Eating and drinking, the same everywhere: hold E with food or a bucket of clean water in hand; when the ring fills, it's gone and your meter goes up right then (a bucket takes the same short hold now; a tap won't drink). Empty hotbar slots can be picked and stepped through again.
  • Fixed: adding wood to a fire no longer makes the boiling bucket on it vanish (it was only hidden; it had been there all along). Hotbar slots, empty ones too, light up under the mouse.
  • Bag fixes: an empty slot can't be picked any more, a bush now says Pick berries, and tide food says Eat. Holding E eats a bite every second even on a slow computer (it used to lag until you let go), and stops when you're full.
